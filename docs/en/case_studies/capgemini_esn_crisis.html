🏛️ I. The Shallow Intermediation Syndrome (R=0)

Traditional IT Service Providers (ESNs) are structured as margin extractors based on information asymmetry. Their business model relies on the formula:

$$\text{Margin} = \text{ADR (Average Daily Rate)} \times \text{Volume of Hours} - \text{Developer Salary}$$

In this equation, the provider has every incentive to maximize the Volume of Hours and minimize actual technical velocity to prolong contracts. The arrival of autonomous engineering agents (Cursor, Antigravity, Devin, Claude Engineer) breaks this model in three ways:

1. Time Compression (The Rupture Effect): What used to take a team of 3 junior developers 100 hours is now executed in 4 hours by 1 First-Hand engineer armed with agents.

2. Devaluation of "Code-Monkey Body Shopping": Clients refuse to pay ADRs of €500+ for basic integration tasks (APIs, forms, refactoring) that AI solves locally.

3. Asphyxiation by Bureaucracy (ANAL Retention): To justify their margins against declining technical volume, providers increase administrative overhead (Agile coaches, Scrum masters, project directors), saturating clients with meetings and Codex reports to mask the lack of real value on the Soil.
